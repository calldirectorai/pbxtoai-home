Why plan now

Businesses may still have older phone services, analog lines, PBX-related services, fax lines, backup lines, and older device connections.

Legacy copper networks are being retired in many areas, and older phone services are increasingly being migrated, repriced, or harder to maintain. Timing varies by location and provider.

Inventory and assessment come first

The first step is understanding what you have—not blindly replacing every line. We document lines, numbers, devices, and dependencies, then recommend a sensible path for each.

Some lines need specialized approaches

Some lines and devices need specialized replacement approaches. We identify them early so they are handled by the right party.

Common Legacy Phone Dependencies

Traditional office phone lines

Main numbers and desk lines that may still ride on older services.

Older PBX systems

On-site equipment that can limit remote answering, routing, and visibility.

Fax lines

Dedicated lines that may be rarely used but still billed every month.

Backup lines

Redundant lines added years ago that may or may not still serve a purpose.

Security and access-control lines

Gates, intercoms, and access-control devices that depend on a phone connection.

These systems may require specialist assessment, approved equipment, power-backup planning, and coordination with the appropriate service provider or monitoring company.

Elevator emergency phones

Emergency communication lines with specific connectivity and power requirements.

These systems may require specialist assessment, approved equipment, power-backup planning, and coordination with the appropriate service provider or monitoring company.

Alarm and fire-related devices

Monitored panels and communicators that rely on specific line types.

These systems may require specialist assessment, approved equipment, power-backup planning, and coordination with the appropriate service provider or monitoring company.
